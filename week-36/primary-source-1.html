Primary Source 36.1
Pliny and Trajan
About this source: Pliny the Younger was a Roman lawyer and governor. About AD 112 he wrote to the emperor Trajan from a province in what is now Turkey, asking how to treat Christians. His letter and the emperor's reply both survive. The passages below are adapted from public-domain translations.
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

In Lesson 36.1 you read how Pliny asked Trajan what to do with Christians. Here are the key lines from Pliny's letter and from Trajan's answer.

Pliny was a careful official, and Trajan was a practical ruler. Ask what each of them cared about, and what each did not say.

EXCERPT 1

They said that the whole of their guilt or error was this: they were accustomed to meet on a fixed day before dawn and sing a hymnHIMA song of praise, usually to God. to Christ as to a god, and to bind themselves by an oathOHTHA solemn promise., not to do any wicked deed, but not to commit theft, robbery, or adulteryuh-DUL-tur-eeBreaking a marriage promise., not to break their word, and not to refuse to return a depositdih-POZ-itSomething left with another person to keep safe. when asked.

Pliny the Younger, Letters, book 10, letter 96 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Pliny was a Roman governor writing to the emperor Trajan from a province in what is now Turkey, about AD 112.
EXCERPT 2

You have followed the right course in your examination of these cases. No fixed rule can be laid down. They are not to be hunted out. If they are brought before you and the charge is proved, they must be punished. But if anyone denies that he is a Christian and proves it by worshiping our gods, he is to be pardonedPAR-dundForgiven and set free from punishment., however suspect his past.

Trajan to Pliny, Letters, book 10, letter 97 (adapted from a public-domain translation). This is the emperor's reply.
🔍 AS YOU READ, NOTICE
  • In the first excerpt, notice when the Christians met and what they promised not to do.
  • Notice that the passage reports what the Christians said, and not what Pliny thought.
  • In the second excerpt, notice what Trajan says about hunting Christians out, and what a person must do to be pardoned.
