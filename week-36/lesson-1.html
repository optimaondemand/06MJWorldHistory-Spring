📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Why did a small movement in a distant province outlast the emperors who tried to stamp it out?

Christianity began in a Roman province under a Roman governor. Today you will see how it spread along the empire's own roads, and why Rome feared it.

📚 A Teacher in Judea

The story begins in Judea, a small, hilly province in the east of the empire. The Jews who lived there worshiped one God and followed God's law, a belief that set them apart from their neighbors. Many hoped that God would send a promised king, an Anointed One, to free them.

Around AD 30, a Jewish teacher from Galilee named JesusJEE-zus of NAZ-uh-rethThe Jewish teacher whom Christians follow as the Son of God. His Hebrew name was Yeshua. began to preach and heal. He taught love of God and neighbor and said the kingdom of God was near. Crowds followed him. According to the Gospels, the leaders of the Temple in Jerusalem feared trouble and handed him to the Roman governor Pontius PilatePON-shus py-LAH-tusThe Roman governor of Judea who sentenced Jesus to death., who ordered him crucified. This was a common Roman punishment for rebels and slaves.

His followers said that Jesus had risen from the dead. Jesus had twelve close followers, called apostles. After his death they began to teach in Jerusalem, and the movement spread to other towns. They called him Jesus the Anointed OneKRIS-tosThe Greek word from which the title Christ comes. It translates the Hebrew word Messiah. (Christos), and soon they were called Christians. This is what Christians believe, and it was the center of their faith. Whatever one thinks of it, a great movement grew from it.

📚 The Faith Takes the Roads

The faith grew from Jewish roots, so it kept the Jewish Scriptures, the belief in one God, and the call to treat others fairly. Rome had made the world easy to travel. Roads and safe seas let messengers move quickly. Greek was understood almost everywhere in the east, so a message in Greek could reach many ears. The GospelsGOS-pulThe 'good news' about Jesus. Also, one of four books in the New Testament that tell his story. and other early Christian writings were written in Greek.

Christians also found their first listeners in the synagoguesSIN-uh-gogA Jewish house of prayer and study.. These Jewish houses of prayer were found in cities all around the Mediterranean, and each one was a ready-made audience for the new message.

📚 Paul

The key figure in this spread was PaulPAWL-us of TAR-susA Jewish teacher and Roman citizen who carried the faith across the empire.. He was a Jew from the city of Tarsus and also a Roman citizen, and at first he fought against the Christians. Then he changed his mind and became their greatest traveler. He sailed from city to city, preaching to Gentiles, who were non-Jews. He wrote letters to the new churches, many of which are now part of the New Testament. Tradition says he was killed in Rome under Nero, as was Peter, another leader of the apostles.

Paul argued that non-Jews did not have to follow all Jewish laws to join the faith. That choice opened the door to people of every nation. Christianity could now become a faith for the whole empire.

Christians also welcomed slaves and free people, women and men, poor and rich, which was unusual. They had no armies, and they did not rebel. They prayed for the emperor, though they would not worship him.

📚 Why Rome Was Uneasy

Rome did not usually mind new gods. It welcomed them all. But Christians would not worship the old gods, and they would not offer a sacrifice to the emperor's spirit. Romans saw the sacrifice as a sign of loyalty. A person who refused seemed to be a bad citizen and a danger to the community. Christians also met in private houses, and rumors spread that they did terrible things in secret.

In AD 64, a great fire destroyed much of Rome. The people blamed Emperor NeroNEER-oh KLAW-dee-us SEE-zerThe last Julio-Claudian emperor, who blamed Christians for the fire of Rome.. The historian Tacitus says Nero turned the blame on the Christians. He had many of them killed in cruel ways. It was the first persecutionper-sih-KYOO-shunCruel treatment of people because of what they believe..

Around AD 112, a governor named PlinyGY-us PLIN-ee-us seh-KUN-dusA Roman governor who wrote to the emperor Trajan to ask what to do about Christians. wrote to the emperor Trajan from a province in what is now Turkey. He asked what to do with Christians. Pliny wrote that they met before dawn, sang hymns, and promised not to steal or lie. He found nothing worse. Still, he made them curse Christ and offer wine and incense to the emperor's image. Those who refused, he put to death. Trajan replied that Pliny should not hunt Christians out. But if someone was brought before him and refused to give way, that person must be punished.

📚 The Persecutions

For about two hundred years, persecution came and went. Most of it was local, started by a mob or a governor. In AD 250, the emperor Decius ordered everyone in the empire to offer sacrifice, and Christians who refused were punished. The last and worst came under Diocletian, as you will see in the next lesson.

Why did the persecutions fail? Many Christians chose to die as martyrsMAR-turA person who is put to death rather than give up their faith. rather than deny their faith. Their courage made a deep impression on the crowds who watched. Christians also cared for the sick and the poor, even during plagues, when others ran away. A pagan could see that Christians were bound by love. Historians cannot say exactly how many Christians there were by AD 300, but perhaps one in ten people in the empire. Rome had tried to crush the faith, and had only helped it grow.
