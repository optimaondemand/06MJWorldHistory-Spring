Primary Source 23.2
Sophocles, Antigone
About this source: Sophocles was an Athenian writer of tragedies who lived from about 496 to 406 BC. Antigone was performed at the City Dionysia around 441 BC. In the play, King Creon has forbidden anyone to bury the body of Polynices, who fought against the city. His sister Antigone buries him anyway. The passages below are adapted from a public-domain translation.
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

In Lesson 23.2 you learned that Greek tragedy made the audience face hard questions without simple answers. Here are the two voices at the center of the play.

The play gives each side strong words. Read both before you decide anything.

PASSAGE

CreonKREE-onThe king of Thebes in the play., the king, declares: "Whoever thinks a friend more important than his own country, I count him as nothing."

Antigone answers him: "I did not think your decreesdih-KREEZOfficial orders given by a ruler. had such power that you, a mortalMOR-tulA human being, who will one day die., could override the unwritten and unshakable laws of the gods. They are not of today or yesterday, but live always, and no one knows from where they came."

Sophocles, Antigone, lines 182 to 183 and 450 to 457 (adapted from a public-domain translation)
🔍 AS YOU READ, NOTICE
  • Notice what Creon says must come before a friend.
  • Notice the two kinds of law in Antigone's answer, and who she says made each one.
  • Neither speaker is a stranger to the other. They are family.
