📚 A League of Free Cities

After the Persian war, many Greek cities worried that Persia would return. In 478 BC, Athens and its allies formed the Delian League. Each member agreed to send ships or money, and they kept the money in a treasury on the island of Delos. Athens led the league because it had the biggest fleet.

At first this was a defense. The league fought Persia and freed Greek cities along the coast. Its general, CimonKY-munThe Athenian general who led the league and favored Sparta. (Kimon), won famous victories. But over time, it changed. Most members stopped sending ships and sent tributeTRIB-yootMoney or goods paid by a weaker state to a stronger one. to Athens instead. Athens used the money to build more ships of its own. A member that tried to leave, like the islands of Naxos and Thasos, was forced to stay. Around 454 BC, the treasury was moved from Delos to Athens. A leagueLEEGA group of states that join together for a shared purpose. of equals had become an empire.

Think about how this looked to a member city. At first, the league was a bargain. Athens protected you from Persia, and you paid your share. But once the danger faded, the bargain changed. You were still paying, and you could not leave. To the members, Athens had stopped being a partner and had become a master.

📚 Silver Owls and Forced Trade

The tribute paid by the cities flowed into Athens as silver. Athens also had its own silver mines at Laurion. Athenian coins, stamped with the owl of the goddess Athena, were trusted all over the Mediterranean. The Athenian empire also controlled trade across the Aegean. Members could not freely choose their trade partners or leave. For many, the tribute was a trade they never agreed to make.

📚 The Democratic Turn

Inside Athens, a quarrel was brewing. Cimon and his supporters, many of them well-off hoplites, wanted to stay friendly with Sparta. A reformer named Ephialtesef-ee-AL-teezThe Athenian reformer who cut the power of the old council in 462 BC. (Ephialtes) and the poorer citizens opposed them. In 462 BC, Ephialtes took away most of the powers of the old Council of the Hill of Ares. These powers were given to the Assembly and to the people's juryJUR-eeA group of citizens who listen to a case and decide it. courts. The poorest citizens, the thetes who rowed the fleet, were now fully part of the system. Soon afterward, Ephialtes was killed. The citizens voted to send Cimon away, and a younger leader took up the cause. His name was PericlesPAIR-ih-kleezThe leader of Athens for about thirty years. (Perikles).

This was a big change. Before, the poor could attend the Assembly, but the old council could overrule them. Now the people, through the Assembly and the juries, had the final word. The rowers who had saved Athens at Salamis could argue that they had earned a larger share in governing it.

👤 FIGURE SPOTLIGHT · Pericles

Pericles was born around 495 BC into a leading Athenian family. He entered politics as a supporter of Ephialtes and rose to become the city's most influential leader. He was not a king. Each year he had to win the trust of the Assembly, and he held the office of general. Pericles was known for his calm manner and his skill as a speaker. The historian Thucydides admired him greatly and said that Athens was a democracy in name but, under Pericles, in practice the rule of the first citizen. He led Athens through its greatest building program and into the early years of a long war with Sparta. He died of the plague in 429 BC. Historians still argue over whether his policies built Athens' greatness or led it toward disaster.

📚 Democracy in Practice

How did Athenian democracy work? Every citizen could attend the Assembly, which met many times a year on a hillside near the Acropolis. A large crowd, perhaps six thousand, would listen to speeches and vote by raising hands. A council of five hundred prepared the business. Many officials were chosen by lotLOTA method of choosing people by chance, like drawing names., which meant drawing names, so that anyone had a chance and no one could buy an office.

The courts were run by large juries of hundreds of citizens chosen by lot. There were no lawyers and no judges. Each side spoke for itself. Under Pericles, citizens were paid a small amount for serving, so that a poor man could afford to take part.

Being chosen by lot meant that a farmer or a craftsman might be a juror one year and a council member the next. Many citizens got a chance to learn how the government worked. In this way, Athens trained its people to rule. This is one reason many people admired Athens, even those who disliked it.

Not everyone liked this. Critics said that it gave power to the crowd, or to whoever spoke best. The playwright Aristophanes mocked it. The philosopher Plato was deeply doubtful of it. Even the historian Thucydides saw its dangers.

These complaints raise a fair question. Is a government of the whole people wise, or only popular? Athenians themselves argued about this. You should keep the question in mind, because later chapters of this story will test it hard.

📚 Who Was Left Out

Athens was not open to everyone. Women could not vote or hold office. Slaves had no rights. The resident foreignersMET-iksFree people from other cities who lived in Athens but were not citizens. (metoikoi) could work and trade, but they could not be citizens, even if their families had lived there for generations. Only free men with Athenian parents counted.

Think about how many people lived in Athens. A large share of them could not take part. Only a minority of the people who lived there were citizens with a vote. This does not erase what Athens achieved. But it reminds us that the Athenian idea of freedom was a freedom for a limited group.

📚 One Question to Carry Forward

Athenian democracy was real, and it was limited. Both Athens and Sparta kept most of the people in their lands out of power. But Athens gave a much larger share of its citizens a real part in ruling. This is the question the next lesson will keep in view: what kind of culture does a city like that produce?
