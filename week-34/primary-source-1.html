Primary Source 34.1
Appian and Plutarch
About this source: Appian was a Greek who wrote about the Roman civil wars about 150 years after they ended. Plutarch was a Greek who wrote a life of Cicero about the same distance in time from his death. The passages below are adapted from public-domain translations, so the wording is close but not exact.
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Lists of names, a reward for every head, and a famous speaker who could not hide. Read how two writers tell what was done to Cicero, and ask what a city must have lost before it allowed this.

In Lesson 34.1 you read how the Triumvirs published lists of enemies. Here is how Appian describes the rewards and the threats, and how Plutarch tells what was done to Cicero.

These are sad and hard passages. Read them to understand how fear and money can change a city.

EXCERPT 1

Whoever kills one of the proscribedproh-SKRYBDNamed on a public list as an enemy who may be killed. and brings the head shall receive, if he is a free man, twenty-five thousand Attic drachmasDRAK-muzGreek silver coins.. If he is a slave, he shall receive his freedom, ten thousand drachmas, and the citizen name of his master. The same rewards shall go to informers. Those who hide or help any of the proscribed shall themselves be put on the list.

Appian, Civil Wars, book 4, chapter 11 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Appian was a Greek who wrote about the Roman civil wars long after they ended.
EXCERPT 2

Antony ordered that Cicero's head and hands be cut off, the hands that had written the speeches against him. When they were brought, he looked on them with joy and laughed, and he ordered them fastened up above the speakers' platform in the ForumFOR-umThe open square at the heart of Rome, where citizens met and speakers gave speeches.. The Romans shuddered at the sight, for they thought they saw not the face of Cicero, but an image of Antony's soul.

Plutarch, Life of Cicero, chapters 48 and 49 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Plutarch was a Greek who wrote about Cicero about a hundred and fifty years after his death.
🔍 AS YOU READ, NOTICE
  • In the first excerpt, notice who is offered a reward, and what each is offered.
  • Notice what happens to anyone who hides a person on the list.
  • In the second excerpt, notice where the head and hands are placed, and how the Romans react.
