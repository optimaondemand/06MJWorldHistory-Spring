📚 A Rich City Across the Sea

Rome had a land army and almost no ships. Carthage had the sea, the gold, and centuries of sailing behind it. Yet when the war came, Rome built a fleet from scratch, drilled its rowers on benches set up on dry land, and invented a way to turn a sea battle into a land fight. Then, at the end of the war, it did something that a Greek historian called unjust.

By 264 BC, Rome held all of Italy south of the Po River. But just across the narrow sea lay a very different power. Carthage, in North Africa, had been founded by Phoenician sailors from Tyre. You met the Phoenicians in the first half of this course. Over the centuries, Carthage became a great trading city. It controlled ports in North Africa, in Spain, on Sardinia, and in western Sicily. Its navy was the best in the western Mediterranean.

Carthage's army was different from Rome's. Carthaginian citizens ran the government and commanded the soldiers, but most of the troops were mercenariesMER-suh-nair-eeA soldier who fights for pay and not for his own country., hired from many lands. They fought well when paid, but they had less reason to die for the city. The Romans called the Carthaginians PunicPYOO-nikThe Roman word for Carthaginian. It comes from the word for Phoenician. (Punicus), and so the wars between them are called the Punic Wars.

📚 Trouble at Messana

The spark came in Sicily. A band of hired soldiers had seized the city of Messana, and a nearby king was attacking them. They asked for help from Carthage and from Rome. Rome's Senate argued. Sending an army across the sea meant war with Carthage. In the end, the Romans voted to help in 264 BC, and the First Punic War began.

Rome had never fought outside Italy. Carthage ruled the sea and had gold. Rome had a big land army but almost no ships. Many observers would have bet on Carthage. Yet the Romans were stubborn, and they did not like to give up.

      📚 Rome Builds a Navy

      The Romans did something remarkable. According to the Greek historian Polybius, they took a captured Carthaginian warship as a model and built about a hundred new ships. While the ships were built, rowers practiced on benches set up on land.

      But Roman crews lacked skill in handling ships. So the Romans invented a trick. They fitted each ship with a boarding bridge called the ravenKOR-vusA boarding bridge with a spike that the Romans used to catch enemy ships. (corvus), a plank with a heavy iron spike. When an enemy ship came close, the Romans dropped the plank onto its deck. The spike held the ships together. Then Roman soldiers rushed across and fought as if they were on land. Think about a Roman farmer who had never seen the sea at work. He was now rowing or fighting on a ship. The raven let him use what he knew best, which was close fighting on solid ground. At Mylae in 260 BC, this trick won the first Roman naval victory.

      📚 Storms, Setbacks, and a Hard Fight

      The war was long and hard. In 256 BC, a Roman army under Regulus landed in Africa, and Carthage was in danger. But Regulus was beaten, and the army was destroyed. Then storms wrecked several Roman fleets, and thousands of sailors drowned. Rome lost more ships to the weather than to Carthage.

      Carthage had a gifted general in Sicily named Hamilcar Barcahuh-MIL-kar BAR-kuhThe Carthaginian general who held out against Rome in Sicily. (Hamilkar Barqa). For years he held out in the mountains with a small force, and he raided the Italian coast. The Roman legionLEE-junThe main unit of the Roman army, with thousands of soldiers.s could not drive him out. Both sides were worn down. Rome's treasury ran dry. Ships were costly, and the sea had already taken so many. Many Romans must have wondered whether the war was worth it. Yet the Senate did not offer terms, because it did not want to quit while Carthage still held Sicily.

      Then the rich citizens of Rome did something notable. They lent money of their own to build yet another fleet. Their reward would come only if Rome won. At the Aegates Islands in 241 BC, the new fleet crushed a Carthaginian fleet that was heavy with supplies. Carthage had no money left to fight on. It asked for peace.

        📚 The Peace and Its Price

        Carthage gave up Sicily and agreed to pay a large sum over many years. Sicily became Rome's first provincePROV-insA land outside Italy ruled by Rome through an official sent from the city.. A Roman governor was sent each year to rule it and collect taxes. This was a new thing for Rome. Until now, it had ruled only in Italy, with allies. A province was different. It was land ruled from outside, and it brought wealth and temptation. The people there did not vote in Rome or send leaders to the Senate. They paid taxes and obeyed the governor, and some governors grew rich on the job.

        Carthage then faced a revolt by its own unpaid mercenaries. The war with them was savage. While Carthage was weak, Rome took advantage. It seized the islands of Sardinia and Corsica, even though it had no good reason. Polybius himself thought this was unjust. Carthage was too weak to fight and gave in.

        Think about how the Carthaginians must have felt. They had fought honorably for twenty-three years. Then, at their weakest moment, Rome took more. Hamilcar Barca, the general who had never lost in Sicily, came home angry. He turned his eyes to Spain and began to plan for the day when Carthage could strike back. His young son, Hannibal, was watching.
