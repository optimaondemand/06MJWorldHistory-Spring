📚 The Fire at Persepolis

He had never lost a battle. His army was loyal, his treasury was full, and the known world lay before him. Then, on the banks of a river in India, his own soldiers refused to go on. What does a conqueror do when the only force that can stop him is the men who follow him?

After Gaugamela, Alexander took Babylon and then Susa, where he found great treasure. In 330 BC, he reached Persepolis, the royal city that the Persian kings had built. He took its treasury, which was huge. Then the great palace was burned. Many fine halls and carvings were lost. Even today, visitors there can see mostly broken columns and stone stairs.

Why? Ancient writers do not agree. One story says that during a drinking party, a woman named Thais urged him to burn it, as payback for the Persian king Xerxes, who had burned the temples of Athens about a hundred and fifty years before. An older general told him not to. Some writers say Alexander soon regretted it. We cannot be sure what really happened, and it is wise to keep that in mind whenever a story is told in only a few sources.

📚 The Death of a King

Darius IIIduh-RY-usThe last great king of Persia, who lost his empire to Alexander. (Dareios) had fled to the east. His own nobles seized him, led by BessusBESS-usThe Persian governor who seized Darius and had him killed. (Bessos), the governor of Bactria. They killed him in 330 BC. Alexander found the body and had it buried with royal honors. He then claimed to be the rightful ruler of Persia and hunted Bessus down.

For the next three years, Alexander fought in Bactria and Sogdiana. This was harder than the big battles. The fighters struck from the mountains and then slipped away. Alexander won in the end, and he married a local noblewoman named Roxana.

📚 A New Kind of King

Alexander began to change. He put on Persian clothing and took Persian officials into his court. He wanted Persians in his army and his government. He had his reasons. He now ruled millions of Persians, and he needed them to accept him. A king who ruled only by the sword would always have to fear revolts. He asked his companions to follow the Persian custom of bowing to the kingpros-kuh-NEE-sisThe Persian custom of bowing low, or lying on the ground, before the king. (proskynesis), a deep bow or even lying on the ground before him.

To the Persians, this was good manners. To Greeks and Macedonians, bowing like that was meant for gods, not men. The Macedonians had followed a king who fought beside them, and now they saw a king who behaved like an eastern ruler. They grew bitter.

In 328 BC, at a drinking party, the quarrel boiled over. CleitusKLY-tusThe Macedonian officer who saved Alexander's life at the Granicus. (Kleitos), the officer who had saved Alexander's life at the Granicus, spoke sharply to him. Alexander grabbed a spear and killed him on the spot. He was filled with horror afterward and is said to have refused to eat for days. It is a warning about what happens when a person with great power cannot control his own temper.

📚 The Mutiny at the River

In 326 BC, Alexander crossed the Indus River and fought a king named Porus, who had war elephants. Alexander won, but it was a hard fight. He wanted to go on farther east, to reach what he thought was the end of the world.

His army had been away from home for eight years. It had marched thousands of miles and was worn out. Many soldiers were wounded. Many had left their homes and families years before. They wanted to see their own land again. The heavy rains of the monsoonmon-SOONA season of very heavy rain in South Asia. soaked the men for weeks. At the river we call the Beas, the army stopped. In a mutinyMYOO-tuh-neeWhen soldiers refuse to obey their commander., the soldiers said they would go no farther. Alexander sulked in his tent for three days. Then he gave in and turned back.

Notice what this means. The man who had never lost a battle was stopped by his own soldiers. Even a great leader needs those who follow. The trip home was grim, too. Many died crossing the desert on the way back.

A few years later, in the lands Alexander had left, a young man named Chandragupta built a great empire in northern India. You will meet his rule later in the course.

📚 Death at Babylon

In June of 323 BC, in Babylon, Alexander fell ill after a banquet. He had a fever for about ten days. He was thirty-two years old. His soldiers filed past his bed, and he could no longer speak. Some writers say that when someone asked him who should rule, he said, "The strongest." We are not sure he said it.

Alexander left no grown heir. His wife, Roxana, was expecting a child. He had marched to the far edge of the known world and made an empire, but he had made no plan for after his death. His generals would soon fight for the pieces.

Here is a question that historians love to ask. What if he had lived? He had plans to explore Arabia and perhaps more. A longer life might have brought more wars, or it might have brought order. We cannot know. But we do know that one man's death, at a fever's whim, changed the course of the world.
