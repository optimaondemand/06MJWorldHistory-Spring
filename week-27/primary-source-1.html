Primary Source 27.1
Arrian, Anabasis of Alexander
About this source: Arrian was a Greek writer of the second century AD, many hundreds of years after Alexander. He built his account from earlier writers who knew Alexander. The two speeches below are his versions of what was said, so they are reconstructions and not records. The passages are adapted from a public-domain translation.
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

In Lesson 27.1 you saw two moments when Alexander met a limit: the quarrel over bowing, and the mutiny at the river. Here are the voices of the men who pressed him.

Neither man is an enemy. Each is speaking as a loyal companion, and each is asking the king to remember something.

EXCERPT 1

CoenusKEE-nusA Macedonian officer who spoke for the army. spoke for the soldiers. "Sire, a ruler must know when to stop. You see how few of the Macedonians and Greeks who set out with you are left. Some were sent home, some died, and some, worn out or wounded, were left behind in the cities. Those of us who remain have no strength for more. All of us long to see our parents, wives, and children again. Go home, sire, and then, if you wish, lead another army."

Arrian, Anabasis of Alexander, book 5, chapter 27 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Arrian wrote many hundreds of years after Alexander.
EXCERPT 2

Callistheneskal-LIS-thih-neezA Greek writer who traveled with Alexander. spoke against the bowing. "Alexander, I think you worthy of every honor that a man can have. But honors for men and honors for gods have always been kept apart. Among the Greeks, no one bows down to a man. You came to add Asia to Greece, and you rule Greeks, so remember Greece."

Arrian, Anabasis of Alexander, book 4, chapter 11 (adapted from a public-domain translation). Arrian wrote many hundreds of years after Alexander.
🔍 AS YOU READ, NOTICE
  • In the first excerpt, notice what Coenus says is left of the army, and what the soldiers want.
  • In the second excerpt, notice the line Callisthenes draws between honors for men and honors for gods.
  • Notice how each speaker is careful to praise Alexander before asking.
