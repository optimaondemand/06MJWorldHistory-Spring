📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How does a great city lose everything in a few years?

After the Peace of Nicias, Athens took one risk after another. Today you will see how pride, a far-off expedition, and Persian money brought the city down.

📚 A Brilliant and Dangerous Young Man

The peace did not last. A rising Athenian named Alcibiadesal-sih-BY-uh-deezA brilliant, daring Athenian who urged the attack on Sicily and later changed sides. (Alkibiades) was soon pushing the city back toward war. He was rich, handsome, and a gifted speaker. After his father died, he had grown up in the house of Pericles. He had also spent time with Socrates. He wanted fame more than anything, and he was willing to take great risks to win it.

Athens and Sparta stayed formally at peace for some years. But each side kept helping its friends and harming the other's, so the peace was more of a pause than an ending.

📚 The Melian Dialogue

In 416 BC, Athens sent a fleet to the island of Melos. The Melians had stayed out of the war. They were colonists from Sparta, but they wanted to be left alone. The Athenians demanded that they join the empire and pay tribute.

Thucydides tells what was said in a famous scene we call the Melian Dialogue. He wrote the speeches as he thought the speakers would have spoken. The Athenians argued that right and wrong matter only between equals. Among those who are not equal, they said, the strong do what they can and the weak accept what they must. The Melians answered that this was unjust, and that they hoped the gods and Sparta would help them.

Sparta did not come. Melos held out, but the Athenians took the island after a siegeSEEJAn attack in which an army surrounds a city and cuts it off until it gives up.. They killed the men and sold the women and children into slavery. Thucydides does not stop to scold. He lets the story speak for itself. Think about what the Athenians said. If the strong may do whatever they can, then no city is safe, not even Athens. Thucydides wants his readers to think about what happens when people decide that justice does not matter. The next year, Athens would learn that the strong do not always stay strong.

📚 The Great Expedition to Sicily

Sicily is a large island far to the west. Some Sicilians asked Athens for help against Syracuse, a powerful city. Alcibiades urged the Athenians to accept. NiciasNISH-ee-usA careful, wealthy Athenian general who helped make the peace of 421 BC. (Nikias) warned that Sicily was too far away and the cost too high. Nicias thought that asking for a huge force would frighten the Athenians. Instead, they gave him exactly what he asked for.

Remember that Sicily was a very long sail from Athens. Food and supplies had to cross the sea, and any mistake would be costly. An attack there was a gamble with the city's whole fleet.

In 415 BC the expeditionek-spuh-DISH-unA long trip by an army or fleet with a goal. sailed. It was one of the largest forces Athens ever sent. Just before it left, the stone statues of Hermes were broken all over Athens in a single night. Enemies of Alcibiades blamed him. He was called home to stand trial, but he ran away to Sparta instead. There he gave the Spartans two pieces of advice. Send a Spartan commander to Syracuse, and set up a fort inside the land of Athens, so the Spartans could raid all year long. Sparta did both.

Syracuse held out. The Athenian fleet was trapped in the harbor and destroyed in 413 BC. The army tried to march away, but it was caught. Thousands of men were taken prisoner and put to work in stone quarries, where many died. Nicias was put to death. Thucydides called it one of the greatest disasters of the whole war. Athens had lost its best ships, thousands of its men, and much of its money all at once.

📚 Persian Gold

Persia had watched the Greeks fight each other for years. After Sicily, many of Athens' allies rebelled. The Persian governor at Sardis offered Sparta money for ships. In return, Persia wanted the Greek cities of Asia Minor back.

Think how strange that is. Sparta had fought Persia at Thermopylae, and now it took Persian gold. The Spartans had never before been a sea power. With Persian money they built a fleet. Lysanderly-SAN-derThe Spartan admiral who won the war at sea. (Lysandros) was a clever Spartan admiral who made friends with a Persian prince and got more money. Athens fought on for years and even won some battles, and Alcibiades came back to lead for a time. But the money gave Sparta what it had always lacked.

    📚 The End at the Hellespont

    In 405 BC, the Athenian fleet camped on the beach at a place called Aegospotami, on the Hellespont. Lysander's fleet lay across the water. Each day the Athenian crews went ashore to find food. Alcibiades, who lived nearby, is said to have warned them to move to a safer place. They ignored him. On the fifth day, Lysander sailed over while the crews were scattered on the shore. He captured almost every ship.

    Now Athens had no fleet. Lysander set up a blockadeblah-KAYDThe use of ships or soldiers to stop food and goods from reaching a city. of Piraeus, and the city began to starve. In 404 BC, Athens gave up. Some of Sparta's allies wanted the city destroyed. Sparta refused, because Athens had served Greece well against Persia. But it ordered Athens to pull down the Long WallsLONG WAWLZTwo walls that ran from Athens to its harbor and kept the road to the sea safe., and the Athenians did so while flute players played.

    Sparta then helped a group of thirty rich Athenians take power. They ruled harshly for about a year. Democracy had lost, and so had the empire. You will see in the next lesson what this did to one famous Athenian, Socrates.
